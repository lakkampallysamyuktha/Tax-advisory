Advance tax is a forecasting discipline, not a payment.

The third instalment is the one that decides whether March is calm or expensive. By the time the fourth instalment comes due, most of the freedom to adjust the estimate has already been spent.

This note walks through the working method the direct tax desk applies through the year — how the estimate is rebuilt from management accounts, how positions are tested before they are relied on, and why a written note travels with every revision of the estimate.

Proper documentation at this stage is crucial. We detail the specific schedules that must be maintained and reviewed, ensuring that when the final return is filed, the underlying rationale for the chosen positions is already thoroughly recorded and defensible.

Advisor Insights

“We are not paid to be certain. We are paid to write down what we are certain of, and what we are not.”

Position notes are usually treated as paperwork. In our work they are the deliverable. The return is a consequence of the note, not the other way around. If the note cannot be written, the return is not ready to be filed.

That is also why the practice does not take a position we would not put our name to in writing. The signature is not a formality. It is the point at which the practice becomes answerable for the position.

On using these

The checklists are working documents, not marketing material.

Each of the guides above is maintained by the desk that owns it, and revised when the underlying procedure changes. Where a guide refers to a statutory date or threshold, the date of last revision is stated at the top of the file.

Guides describe the practice's internal procedure. They are not a substitute for engagement-specific advice.

Editorial Standards

How the Journal is written.

Three rules, applied to every note published here.

Journal notes are written by the desk that worked the matter. Where a note describes a general position, the applicable law is stated. Where it describes the practice's own method, that is stated instead.

Every note carries a named author. The name at the bottom of a note is the advisor who wrote it and who is answerable for its content. Where a note draws on work by more than one desk, the lead desk's author signs it.

Confidence is stated in writing. Where a position is clear, the note says so. Where a position is arguable, the note says that, and describes what would make it fail. Where a position depends on facts the practice does not yet have, the note says that too, and does not opine.

Client work is never quoted without permission. Illustrations are drawn from anonymised or synthetic examples. Where a note describes the outcome of a matter, the note is marked as an illustrative example and is not presented as a client result.
