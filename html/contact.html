Document Checklist

What to send, by category.

The lists below describe the standing intake for each type of matter. Engagement-specific lists are issued with the written scope.

Send what exists. Where a document is missing, note it — the desk will tell you whether it is required for the position or merely helpful.

Files should be shared through the secure intake link. Reference numbers are issued on receipt.

Standing intake list for individual return, capital gains and NRI matters.

  • PAN and Aadhaar copies
  • Bank statements for the period in question
  • Form 26AS and AIS for the relevant year
  • Prior year return and computation
  • Investment, insurance and loan proofs
  • Capital gains: contract notes and purchase records

Standing intake list for corporate return preparation, assessment and filing.

  • Audited or management accounts for the year
  • Trial balance and general ledger extract
  • Prior year return, computation and working papers
  • Form 26AS, AIS and TDS returns filed
  • Fixed asset register and depreciation schedule
  • Copies of any notices received

Standing intake list for GST reconciliation, refund and reply matters.

  • GST registration certificate and amendments
  • Sales and purchase registers for the period
  • Returns filed (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, annual return)
  • Input credit ledger and reversal working
  • Any departmental notices or queries
  • Bank statements supporting refund claims

Standing intake list for statutory, internal and tax audit engagements.

  • Trial balance, general ledger and sub-ledgers
  • Bank statements and reconciliations for the year
  • Fixed asset register with additions and disposals
  • Statutory registers (minutes, share register, charges)
  • Prior year audit file and management letter
  • Related party transactions and disclosure notes

Standing intake list for entity, transaction and cross-border matters.

  • Constitutional documents and group structure chart
  • Draft transaction documents, if available
  • Intercompany agreements and pricing policy
  • Treaty residency certificates for entities involved
  • Prior transfer pricing study and local file
  • Succession documents where relevant

From you

What helps us reply usefully.

A first message does not need to be long. It does need to tell us where to look.

  • The structure — individual, entity or group
  • The deadline, if there is one
  • What has already been filed or asked
  • The specific question you want answered
  • Whether another advisor is already involved

Before You Write

The first message is easier than it looks.

Most first messages that go unanswered are unanswered for the same reason: they describe the whole situation rather than the question. The practice is built to work from a question.

Two paragraphs are usually enough. One paragraph on the structure and the deadline. One paragraph on what has been filed, asked or left unanswered. That is the whole intake.

  • 01 Write the question first. The background reads better when it is supporting something specific.
  • 02 Mention deadlines by date. A due date changes which desk takes the file.
  • 03 Say whether another advisor is already involved. It shapes how the file is opened.
  • 04 Do not attach sensitive documents yet. Wait for the secure intake link.
