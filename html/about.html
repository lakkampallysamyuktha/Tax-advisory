The Founding Years

How the register started.

Origins

A compliance desk that kept a private set of notes.

Stackly began as a small compliance desk serving closely held businesses in and around Salem. The work was filing calendars, reconciliations and replies to departmental queries. What separated it from other desks at the time was a habit: every position, however small, was written down with the reasoning behind it and the date it was taken.

The Shift

Clients began asking for the notes, not just the return.

Within a few years, clients started requesting the working papers themselves — not to file, but to understand. Finance teams used them to brief their boards. Some used them across audit cycles. The practice formalised the habit: a written position note accompanies every material conclusion, as a matter of course.

The Present

Five desks, one documentary standard.

The practice now runs five advisory desks under a single documentary standard. Direct tax, GST, audit, structuring and regulatory. Each desk keeps its own register. All five registers obey the same rule: if the reasoning is not written, the position is not taken.

Mandate & Charter

What we will and will not do.

The charter is deliberately narrow. It is what allows the practice to hold a position under review.

Within the mandate

Positions we will take, and stand behind.

We advise on tax and compliance questions where the applicable law can be identified, the facts can be established and the reasoning can be recorded. Where any of those three is missing, we say so, and we either obtain what is missing or decline the position.

  • Direct tax computation, filing and representation
  • GST registration, reconciliation and refunds
  • Statutory, internal and tax audit engagement
  • Structuring advice supported by written reasoning
  • Regulatory filings with a documented calendar
Outside the mandate

What we will not do — regardless of instruction.

Certain instructions are declined on principle. They are not negotiable, they do not depend on the size of the engagement, and they do not depend on who is asking. This is stated here so that no prospective client mistakes the practice's willingness to argue for a willingness to overreach.

  • Positions unsupported by identifiable authority
  • Filing documents we know to be inaccurate
  • Backdating of any document or instrument
  • Arrangements whose sole purpose is concealment
  • Assurance of outcomes dependent on a third party

How We Operate

Five rules that do not change with scale.

Each principle carries an operating consequence. Read together, they describe how the practice behaves when it is under pressure.

Every material conclusion is reduced to a short written note before any document is filed. The note records the facts relied upon, the provision applied, the reasoning adopted and the residual risk if the position is not accepted.

This is not a formality. It is how the practice distinguishes between positions it will defend and positions it will revise. If a note cannot be written, the position is not yet ready.

Consequence · No filing is prepared without a signed position note.

Working papers are assembled as the work happens. They are not reconstructed at year-end, and they are not assembled only when a notice arrives. Each paper carries a source reference and a preparer's mark.

The effect is that any file can be handed to a second advisor who has never seen it before, and read cold, without a call.

Consequence · Working papers exist before submission, not after.

Every deadline in an engagement is owned by a specific advisor by name. A shared calendar is a reminder; an owner is a control. Where a deadline is at risk, the client is informed before it is missed, not after.

Consequence · Exceptions are logged and reviewed at the practice level.

Where a position is clear, we say so. Where a position is arguable, we say so, and we describe what would make it fail. Where a position depends on facts we do not yet have, we say that too, and we do not opine until we do.

Consequence · Every written advice states its own confidence and its own limits.

Before any material filing, a second advisor reads the file as though seeing it for the first time. They record what they could not establish, what they would ask, and what they would want answered before signing.

Consequence · Every material file carries a signed review memo.

Dates That Mattered

A register of turning points.

Years below mark the practice's own structure, not client outcomes. They record moments where the working method changed.

  1. Year one

    The compliance desk opens.

    A single desk serving closely held businesses in Salem and the surrounding districts, working primarily on direct tax filings and reply drafting.

  2. Year three

    Position notes become standard.

    The written-note habit, previously informal, is formalised. Every material conclusion now carries its own short note, and every note carries a date.

  3. Year five

    The audit desk is added.

    Statutory and internal audit work begins, using the same documentary standard. The two desks begin sharing a single review process.

  4. Year eight

    Second-read review introduced.

    A second advisor is assigned to every material file for a cold read. The review memo becomes part of the standard file set.

  5. Year eleven

    Structuring and regulatory desks open.

    Practice expands into planning, succession and cross-border reporting. The five-desk structure takes its present shape.

  6. Present

    Five desks, one documentary standard.

    The practice continues to operate under a single standard across all five desks. Scope grows; the standard does not change.

How the Desks Connect

Five desks, one review line.

Desks are separate in expertise and shared in method. Every file crosses the same review line before it is filed.

Practice Floor

The desk is a place, not a role — which is why reviews work.

Advisors sit with their desks, and desks sit with each other. When a file crosses from one desk to another for review, it crosses a physical boundary. That has proven useful: it makes the second read a genuine second read, and not a formality performed by the same person on a different day.

  • GSTRegistration, reconciliation, refunds, replies

Review Line

A single standard across all five desks.

Each desk keeps its own register. All five registers obey the same review line. Every material file is read by an advisor from a different desk before sign-off.

Sign-off

Named leads, personal accountability.

Each desk has a named lead. The lead's name appears on the file. If the file is later questioned, the lead answers for it.

Register

Every desk keeps its own register.

Deadlines, exceptions and open items are recorded per desk. The practice reviews registers collectively on a fixed cycle.

Escalation

Open questions have an owner by name.

Nothing is left open-ended. Every question raised in review is either answered, escalated or declined — with a date attached.

Standards We Hold

What we hold ourselves to.

Four standards applied uniformly. Each is stated in the terms the practice is willing to be measured against.

Working papers that survive a cold read.

A working paper is not a summary. It is a reproducible path to the number. Anyone reading it should be able to follow the same steps and reach the same conclusion without a conversation.

  • ContentFacts, provision, reasoning, residual risk
  • FormatShort form; source reference on each row
  • TimingPrepared alongside the work, not after
  • Sign-offPreparer mark and review mark

Every figure traces to a document.

Figures are not entered without a source. Where a source is not available, the gap is recorded rather than filled. Where a figure is estimated, the estimate is described and dated.

  • RuleNo figure is entered without a source reference
  • ExceptionsEstimates described in writing, signed by the lead
  • ReconciliationLedger to return reconciled before filing
  • TraceabilityAny figure can be traced back through the file

A second read, by someone who was not there.

Material files are read by an advisor from a different desk before sign-off. The reviewer records what they could not establish, what they would ask, and what they would want answered before signing.

  • TriggerAll material filings and representations
  • ReviewerAn advisor from a different desk
  • OutputWritten review memo, attached to the file
  • RecordReviewer name and date on every memo

Records are kept for as long as they may be needed.

Working papers, position notes, review memos and filing copies are retained beyond the statutory minimum. Retention is defined per engagement, in writing, at the outset.

  • MinimumBeyond the applicable statutory window
  • FormatIndexed digital archive plus physical originals
  • AccessClient may request copies of their own file
  • DeletionBy written instruction only, after the window closes
